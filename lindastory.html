Lasting Impressions New Orleans Reunion

From an earlier version of the association's website, saved 27 April 2006.

Lasting Impressions

On the final leg of our two day journey from Puerto Vallarta to New Orleans my apprehensions were growing. I did not know what I was getting myself into. After all this was my first Marine Ball to attend, but I was in for many pleasant surprises - the first being on TWA. As our plane was approaching New Orleans the steward approached Calvin and handed him a beautiful bottle of champagne as he said, "HERE'S TO THE MARINES!"

The Hilton lobby was a maze of Marines - how could I tell? They were either wearing baseball caps, jackets, belt buckles, or all the above with the Marine emblem. We were told the orientation welcome party was at 4:30 sharp (and when they say Sharp, they mean it!) After unpacking, chilling our champagne in our wastepaper basket filled with ice, we headed to the 29th floor - the top of the Hilton. I was expecting chairs in a row with a dutiful speaker to lull us to sleep. Wrong!

We were greeted with a spectacular panoramic view of the Mississippi River, tug boats and all. The Horizons Lounge was already filled with Marines and their wives, all the guys huddling in the middle of the lower level where tables of Marine gear was for sale. All shopped 'til they dropped while the wives were dutifully standing in the background or mingling at one of two bars, placed at either end on the second level. In the middle on top level was a light buffet. I silently laughed to myself as I observed that the food consisted of what I call, rabbit platters - huge trays filled with carrots, celery, cucumbers, dips, plus other trays of fruits and cheeses. Light healthy food wouldn't you say for these 150+ plus Marine hunks!

I realized I wasn't going to see Calvin for awhile so I approached an attractive wife and said, "Well it's obvious we have two things in common - we're both Marine wives and neither of us know anyone here!" We instantly became fast new best friends. The evening ended about nine on an upbeat note with lots of laughs and good cheer.

Calvin and I then proceeded to the famous French Quarter with full intentions of eating a gourmet Cajun dinner. However our first stop was our only stop - Monteleone Hotel and the famous Carousel Bar. The old doorman greeted us and said to Calvin -"I know you!" That was the beginning - everywhere we went someone in some bar knew him! Now I know where he was all the time when he lived there as a single man! Ha!

After going round and round on the Carousel ,listening to a fabulous pianist and saluting my Dad (this was his favorite hotel and bar) we headed back to the hotel and called it a night at 2 a.m.

Thursday, Nov.9th all 200+ Marines were in the lobby at 6 a.m. sharp (sin for most wives ) to attend a sunrise memorial service at Woldenberg Park on the Mississippi River, followed by a morning meeting. I was told the Hilton staff personally welcomed them and the full Marine Band played for them!

At 11:00 the Marines and wives assembled outside the Hilton entry to proceed to the Riverboat Natchez for our luncheon cruise. What a sight I'm sure we made - there were most likely a total of 500+ of us in the processional as we made our 20 minute walk along the wharf. As we passed one small street jazz band after another - they tooted their horns with the Marine song in honor of our guys! A great time was had by all with marvelous Cajun food (finally), drinks and a live Dixie Jazz band. I must insert here - this was my first experience with a Cajun style Bloody Mary - if you want a real eye opener, try it sometime! Rim a glass with tobasco sauce, dip in salt - add vodka and a wicked spicy mix - insert a couple of hot marinated fresh green beans (in place of celery) Wow!

We had the rest of the day and evening free so headed to the historic French Quarters once more for some serious sightseeing. Our 1st. stop was 301 Royal for a photo of our good friends, landmark (she and her husband once owned a world renowned antique store here.) There is now another antique store called Hooovers- not as impressive as the pictures I've seen of theirs! We walked up the block with Calvin pointing out where he shopped, where he lived - quite an impressive part of Royal, next to the Cornstalk Hotel (still as popular as ever) and ended up at Lafittes Blacksmith shop, one of the oldest bars in New Orleans. Our last stop - Pat O'Briens,where Calvin and pianist, Barbara Bennett renewed old friendship (she' a legend- having been at O'Briens for 42 years!) We ended our day that night at Commander's Palace for our belated anniversary dinner. We just thought we'd ended it - we couldn't resist Harrah's Casino across from the hotel - so made a quick stop - only allowing ourselves $40 each - we each proceeded to go through most of our coins at the slot machines. I finally wandered over to the blackjack tables and won some of our losses back. More on Calvin's winnings later!

Friday, Nov. 10 the Marines were up and at it 8 o'clock sharp for another Association Business Meeting - this time lasting until 1:00 p.m. (or as they say-1300). I was dutifully waiting for Calvin in the lobby ready to go do serious shopping. We took off on the little red and gold train outside our hotel and went to Jackson Square and the old French Market - our serious shopping consisted of a few trinkets and spices. Then off we went to the famous Tujague's, established in 1856, for a light bite and ending our day once again at Pat O'Brien's. We hurried back to the hotel to get ready for the Marine Ball, starting at 5:30 sharp! (17:30).

I had been forewarned that this event would blow me over but never to the extent it did. We entered a promenade entry area, flanked by two open grand ballrooms - everywhere you turned were active Marines in full dress blues, Marines in tuxedos draped with medals of honor and fabulously gowned women. We were immediately issued 2 VIP invitations by Ray Raymond (who by the way was one of the main coordinators of this event and the reason we went!) and proceeded up the escalator to the General's private cocktail party. It was quite an honor for us both to be included and have the opportunity to meet these dignitaries.

At 6:30 all 3,000 people were seated in the two ballrooms (the enlisted men having their own.) I never dreamed a dinner for 3,000 people could be such a gourmet experience but the Hilton proved me wrong. I must share our menu.Each round table of ten had three different types of wines chilled in the middle. Each table had their own waiter. The first course was a delightful seafood bisque, piping hot and rich! The second course was a beautiful green salad tossed at the table and served on chilled plates (I was impressed!!). The main course was a mouth watering filet of beef marchand duvin. Dessert was a praline parfait in a chocolate cup. Whoever had room was served a slice of the Marine Corps Birthday Cake (which I might add was the center of attention as one entered the Ball as it stood over 6 feet high!)

After a delightful dinner, a special ceremony was presented. This was quite emotional for Calvin, the 3rd Recon Association and their wives, as they were given a standing ovation by the General's Staff and the Officer's Corps. Our emotions didn't end here as the 75+ plus Marine Band came marching into our ballroom - they are so handsome in their blues, so precise in their sharp moves and so overpowering with their music. I don't believe there was a dry eye in the room .The cake cutting ceremony came next, followed by Lt. Gen. Cooper's moving speech. The finale was....a full dance band entertaining until 1:00 a.m. The 225 Year Marine Birthday Ball will always be a memorable experience for me that I will never forget!

Saturday, Nov.11 - Veterans Day brought our guys out in force at 9:00 a.m. waiting and ready for the 3rd Recon Parade. They actually marched with police escorts down Canal Street, ending at the historical D -Day Museum.

After the parade we once again set off for the French Market via our train for last minute sight seeing, shopping and of course eating, this time at the actual French Market Restaurant across from the Cafe Du Monde.You guessed it, we ended up at Pat O'Briens - where else? Before entering, though I temporarily lost Calvin, I turned around to say something and he wasn't with me. I looked across the street and heard him running towards an electrician's truck, yelling 'Happy Birthday Jarhead!" I thought he'd lost his mind. When he came back to me-I said, "What was that all
about?" He replied, "He had a Marine sticker on the window of his truck and all Marines stick together and congratulate each other on Veterans Day!"

Once again we had to be ready for the big gala at Harrah's Casino for the special 3rd Recon Battalion Banquet. Fluffed and puffed once more, this time in cocktail attire, a group of us walked to our last function together. The Mansion Ballroom at Harrah's was truly breathtaking! Huge murals lined the walls and round tables for kings. This time however
the attendance was greatly reduced to a select 500+ people. The Hilton catered the affair so again we had a delightful dinner. This was followed by speeches, raffle prizes and picture taking. I found this evening very moving as they introduced the Dad's of sons who had been killed in war. One particular brother came on behalf of his POW Brother. It was remarkable how brave and courageous these family members were and accepted their plaques with dignity. One other person my heart went out to, a very attractive lady I had noticed throughout the 4 day event. She was presented a special plaque as her husband had just died prior to the Marine Ball and she was there to represent him. I don't think I would have been as composed. Speaking of death. We were told that just in the last years 99 Marines have died of
cancer due to a special poisonous spray used in Vietnam. This is quite disturbing to me.

On a lighter note - a Colonel spoke who had the longest tenure in the Marines of anyone present - 44 years to be exact -he just stepped down from active duty. He gave a dynamic speech, talking about many of his ventures with the Marine Corps. He said his wife was always at his side but one day looked at him and said,"I do believe you love the Marine Corps more than you do me! "His reply- "Honey, I love you lots more than the Army, Airforce or Navy!"

The evening came to an end much too fast so Calvin and I decided to try our luck once again at gambling, this time black jack only. I immediately knew this wasn't my lucky night so turned my pile of chips over to Calvin, who was then on a roll. Thirty minutes later we left with $225 (from our one $100 to start) Not bad for amateurs! Back at the hotel bar everyone was exchanging cards, saying farewells and generally summing up their last four days.

Sunday, Nov.12th found us packed and ready to enjoy our last day in the Crescent City (our plane didn't leave until 8 p.m.). We invited our new friends, Bob and Glenise up to enjoy the champagne (still iced down in the wastebasket). I am sure our maid had some laughs over this. After draining the bottle of bubbly we took our train to the French Quarters and walked to Brennan's for breakfast. Bob and Glenise joined us for a sumptuous meal, another bottle of champagne and three hours later we walked off our food. We retraced all our steps from the past days, starting with one cordial only at the Carousel Bar, then up Royal, venturing back to Lafitte's where this time, we listened to a great blues pianist. Pat O'Brien's was our last stop before the four of us parted ways and one last drink for the Marines. When several
guys heard Calvin and Bob were Marines they started treating them (and us) to drinks on them, saying, Here's to the Marines - drinks on me! " The four of us had teary eyes.

My summation of the Marine Ball week - unbelievable - mind boggling - out if this world fantastic - to see and witness such camaraderie (loyalty and warmth among these Marines), such powerful bonding and complete dedication to their country and the Marine Corps...it makes a wife very proud to be her husband's mate. I'm also quite proud to know my husband's Special Forces 3rd Recon was commemorated at the Marine Ball because they were the most decorated and one of the most elite units in the Vietnam War. And what makes me the most proud is they don't brag or talk about it. As
Calvin says, "My medals are great- but nothing is as meaningful to me as to have brought all my men home safe and alive."

Calvin served with the 3rd Reconnaissance Battalion in 1961-1962 with the 1st Marine Brigade - FMF - BRAVO RECON, Hawaii and with BRAVO RECON 1965-1966 in Vietnam in between years he served as an instructor at Camp Horno, NCO School at Camp Pendleton, Calif. He also served as an instructor at the USMC MARINE CORPS MOUNTAIN TRAINING CENTER Bridgeport, Calif. When he was discharged from the Marines he was serving with the 2nd Reconnaissance Battalion at Camp LeJeune, N.C.

SEMPER FI, Linda Bockbrader

In the Marine traditions of pride, dignity and honor, the term "Semper Fi" is reserved for those that served and stands for Always Faithful. In Linda's case, as a loving wife of a Marine, she went through boot camp and advanced training during the New Orleans Reunion, her use of the term is entirely appropriate. Linda thanks for Calvin's support and also for all of us.

"Scribe"
