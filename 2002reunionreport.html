Reunions

LZDC Reunion After Action Report

From an earlier version of the association's website, saved 16 November 2004.

Alpha Company - 3rd Recon, along with other Marines gathered in Washington, DC over the Veterans Day weekend. The focal point was to commemorate the 20th Anniversary of the Vietnam Memorial WALL, along with celebrating our Brotherhood and our friendship with other Marines. Joining our group were six Medal of Honor recipients, from WWII to the Vietnam War. Our attendance was outstanding. Many of our Marines marked their first reunion attendance. It is a special time when we are able to re-connect with our brothers, after all these years, and this gathering showed our "Brotherhood" is strong. There were numerous other Veteran's Reunion’s during this special weekend held throughout the Washington area. There was also a contingent of Marines from “Pop-a-Smoke”, our Aircrew Brothers, at The Wall, providing their camaraderie to all from their distinctive yellow tent.

After the first nights reception at the hotel, the early morning bus trip launched us quickly into our agenda with a group visit to The Vietnam Veterans Memorial. We placed a wreath at the Wall which proceeded a very emotional heartfelt two hour visit. Our group supplied approximately twenty readers to assist in the "Reading Of Names". The reading of names took four days to complete by Veterans and Family members taking turns in the recitation of over 58,000 lost during the Vietnam War. Those of us that participated felt honored to do so. Our next stop was a luncheon at the Fort Meyers Officers Club. The dining experience was very enjoyable and the food far surpassed our memories from the past. We then journeyed to The Tomb Of The Unknown where we were included in a formal wreath laying ceremony at The Tomb. Once again, a very emotional experience, especially for those visiting the first time.

The next morning we traveled to the Iwo Jima Memorial. Each year the Marine Corps celebrates it’s birthday with a formal ceremony at the Memorial. This years included the current and 32nd Commandant of the Marine Corps General Jones, the President’s Own Band, and both companies of Marines from Eighth & I. Needless to say there were lots of “Dress Blues”. The Guest speaker was the 28th Commandant, General PX Kelley, retired. The ceremony and address's by General's Jones and Kelley inspired everyone. Next on the agenda was a cruise luncheon on the Potomac River aboard the "Dandy". Since the boat was “ours” for a few hours, this venue offered a great opportunity to mingle and enjoy the DC fall scenery. Our hotel that evening was host to the Commandant’s Ball, which made the lobby an interesting site with so many senior officers of the Corps present.

The next morning brought the dawn of the 227th Birthday of the Corps. Alpha Company held a brief business meeting that morning. Topics discussed included the election of Company Officers, an update on the efforts of JTFFA to account for our missing from Teams Striker, Breaker, and Ronald Loganecker who perished during Operation Hastings. Miscellaneous other items were discussed with the location of our next reunion heading the list.

After reconnecting with our “Pop-a-Smoke” brothers earlier in the weekend, it was unanimously voted to have our next Alpha Company reunion in Reno, NV during July, 2004. This offered a more cost effective choice for our members, given the more expensive venues at both New Orleans and Washington, DC. In addition, our “Pop-a-Smoke” brothers are a special breed and we all felt that it was time to have an opportunity to spend more quality time with them.

Following the meeting, the buses left for another heartfelt trip to the WALL. We participated in a small parade and wreath laying ceremony with the “Pop-a-Smoke” group to commemorate those lost in Vietnam on their aircraft.

That evening was our Marine Corps. Birthday Ball. Our honored guests were recognized and thanked for their attendance with us. Very memorable tributes were presented by General's Kelley and Weise. General Weise surprised most all in attendance when he read a proposed citation for the awarding the Bronze Star to Alpha Company President Jeff Savelkoul for his brave acts of heroism during the “Striker” patrol in 1967.

Our guest speaker, coincidentally, was retired Commandant General PX Kelley. Many of us were aware of Gen. Kelly’s active duty contributions to the Corps. He remains active, currently serving as Chairman of the WWII Memorial Committee, appointed by President Bush, but his personalized speech to our group truly impacted every attendee.

The final day marked the actual 20th Anniversary of the Vietnam Memorial WALL ceremony. Our buses shuttled members between the hotel and the WALL every 30 minutes. A huge gathering of over 20,000 attended. Our group presented a seventy pound granite rock at the Wall, with all Marine attendees signing. The stone was engraved with the Marine Corps and Corpsman’s emblems on its face. This commemorative item will find it’s way to the Vietnam Memorial Museum, where similar items have been collected from the Wall and become a permanent record.

In summary, a GREAT reunion, with many lasting memories. There were lots of heartfelt joy, old war stories, laughter and tears shared by all.

As a end note, Ray Raymond received a phone message and the contents of the message is below.

We received a call from Gen Kelley on 11/12 and he said that he "felt so honored to have been with us on this occasion". Gen Kelley went on to say that "he has been to so many of these fancy affairs and has been left cold by them". In the case of our reunion Gen. Kelley expressed that "the warmth, sincerity, and the genuine emotion in the room was such that it would remain with him for a long, long time". He went on to say that he "apologized that his remarks were off the cuff but felt that we deserved more than just remarks from "a General"”. The General closed his message saying that “ it pleased him so much to be with the real people that make us look good even when we might not deserve it”.

Truly heartfelt remarks from a great man. Ray Raymond

Special Thanks to Don Schwinn and Ray Raymond for this report.
